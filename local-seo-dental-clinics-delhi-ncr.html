Dental Clinic SEO

Why new and established dental clinics both lose patients to competitors on Google Maps

Dental search is appointment-intent search — someone is choosing a provider, not browsing. They open Google Maps, compare the top 3 by rating and review count, and book. A new clinic with excellent care but 3 reviews loses to an established practice with 200, even when the newer clinic would deliver a better experience. The gap isn't quality. It's visibility.

The most common problem we find is a generic GBP category — "Medical Clinic" or "Health" instead of "Dentist" or a treatment-specific category like "Cosmetic Dentist" or "Orthodontist". The second most common: no structured review system, so genuinely satisfied patients simply never get asked. Both are fixable without touching the quality of care that got those patients satisfied in the first place.

Common Questions

Questions from dental clinic owners

Why isn't my dental clinic showing up for "dentist near me" searches?

The three most common causes: a generic GBP category ("Medical Clinic" or "Health" instead of "Dentist" or "Cosmetic Dentist"), too few recent Google reviews compared to established competitors, and incomplete profile information. New clinics competing against practices with years of accumulated reviews face a real gap, but it's closeable with the right category and a structured review system.

We're a new clinic with no online presence. How long until we're visible?

Starting from zero is more common than you'd think, and it's not necessarily slower to fix than an established profile with bad data — sometimes it's faster, because there's nothing conflicting to correct. Delhi Dental Care went from no visibility at all to #1 on Google Maps in 11 weeks. We give an honest, category-specific timeline during the free audit rather than a generic promise.

What Google Business Profile category should a dental clinic use?

"Dentist" as the primary category for general practices, with specific secondary categories added for services you actually offer — "Cosmetic Dentist", "Orthodontist", "Pediatric Dentist", "Dental Implants Periodontist" — matched to what patients search for. Listing under a broad "Medical Clinic" or "Health" category is the single most common mistake we find.

How do you generate patient reviews without violating healthcare marketing norms?

A structured, post-appointment follow-up asking every patient for a genuine review — not selective, not incentivised. We never draft reviews on a patient's behalf or request reviews referencing specific treatment outcomes in ways that could raise privacy concerns. The goal is real patient volume and satisfaction converted into a visible signal.

Is Local SEO worth it if we already get patients from doctor referrals?

Referrals and search are different acquisition channels, not competing ones — most growing practices need both. Referral volume is capped by your existing network; Google Maps visibility captures every new-to-the-area patient and every referral-less search that a referral network simply can't reach.
