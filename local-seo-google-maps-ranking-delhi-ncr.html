How It Actually Works

What determines your Google Maps position

Google ranks the map pack — the 3 businesses shown with a map at the top of a local search — on three broad factors: relevance, distance, and prominence. Relevance is how well your Google Business Profile matches what the searcher typed, which is why category accuracy matters more than most business owners expect. Distance is how close your listed location is to the searcher. Prominence is a mix of your review signals, citation consistency, and how complete and active your profile is.

None of these are things a website redesign fixes on its own. A business can have a fast, well-written website and still be invisible in the map pack, because the map pack is driven primarily by Google Business Profile signals, not website content. This is the single most common misunderstanding we see during the free audit — business owners who've invested in their website and assume that's what's holding rankings back, when the actual gap is in GBP, citations, or reviews.

The three inputs into map pack ranking

Relevance. How precisely your GBP category and business description match what someone searched — a generic category is the single most common cause of poor relevance.

Distance. How close your business location is to the person searching — the reason a business can rank #1 in one part of a city and not appear at all a few kilometres away.

Prominence. Review count and recency, citation consistency across directories, and overall profile completeness — the signals that tell Google your business is real, active, and trustworthy.
A Common Point of Confusion

Ranking #1 organically doesn't mean ranking in the map pack

A local search results page usually shows two separate rankings stacked on top of each other: the map pack (3 businesses with a map, driven by GBP signals) and the organic results below it (blue links, driven by website content and backlinks). These are ranked independently. A business can sit at position one organically and still not appear in the map pack at all — or the reverse.

For most local service businesses, the map pack is where the overwhelming majority of calls and enquiries come from, because it sits above the fold with a map, phone number, and directions one tap away. That's why our engagements prioritise GBP, citations, and reviews first — the levers that move the map pack — rather than starting with website content that primarily affects the organic results underneath it.

Common Questions

Questions about local SEO and Google Maps ranking

What's the difference between SEO and local SEO?

Regular SEO tries to rank a website in general organic results, often against national competition. Local SEO ranks a specific business location for searches with local intent — the result that matters most for these searches is the Google Maps 3-pack, not the organic links below it. A business can have a well-optimised website and still be invisible in the map pack, because the map pack is driven primarily by Google Business Profile, not website content.

What's the difference between ranking organically and ranking in the map pack?

They're ranked by different signals. The map pack weighs GBP completeness, category accuracy, proximity, and reviews most heavily. Organic ranking weighs website content, backlinks, and technical SEO more heavily. Most local service businesses get the overwhelming majority of their calls from the map pack, which is why GBP work is usually the highest-leverage starting point.

How long does local SEO take to show results in Delhi NCR?

Most businesses see measurable movement within 4-8 weeks of the core fixes. Full compounding results typically build over 3 months, which is our minimum engagement — month 1 builds the foundation, months 2-3 produce the movement. Competitiveness of your category and zone changes the exact timeline. Every engagement includes a 60-day progress guarantee.

Do I need a website to rank on Google Maps?

Not strictly — GBP can rank in the map pack with a minimal or even no linked website, because map pack ranking depends far more on GBP signals than website content. A website still matters for conversion once someone clicks through and for organic visibility beyond the map pack, but it's a supporting signal, not the primary lever, when the core problem is map pack visibility.

Does local SEO work for every type of business?

It works well for businesses that serve a defined local area and rely on inbound search — trades, clinics, home services, legal and professional services, salons, and similar categories. It's not the right fit for e-commerce with no physical service area, or businesses in their first few months without a track record Google can weigh. We say this directly during the free audit.

Why is a competitor with fewer reviews ranking above me?

Review count is only one input into map pack ranking, alongside proximity, category accuracy, GBP completeness, and citation consistency.
