Why It's Different

More locations means more ways to accidentally compete with yourself

A single-location business has one Google Business Profile, one set of citations, one review base to build. A multi-location business has all of that multiplied — and every one of those multiplications needs to be genuinely distinct, not copy-pasted with a different city name swapped in. Google can suppress near-duplicate listings or pages that appear to be targeting the same search rather than serving it, which means a lazily-structured multi-location setup can actively hurt every location involved, not just fail to help them.

The underlying ranking factors don't change — relevance, distance, and prominence still determine each location's individual Maps position. What changes is the structural discipline needed to give each location its own clean version of those signals, without them cannibalising each other.

Common Questions

Questions about multi-location local SEO

Do I need a separate Google Business Profile for each location?

Yes, for each genuinely separate physical location. What you don't need is a separate profile per technician or per service if you operate from one location and travel to customers — that's a service-area business configuration, not a multi-location one. Getting this distinction wrong is one of the most common structural mistakes we find.

How do you stop multiple locations from competing against each other in search?

By giving each location genuinely distinct signals — unique descriptions, location-specific photos, distinct service area configuration, and different content rather than a city name swapped into a template. Google can suppress near-duplicate listings that appear to compete for the same search rather than serve it, so differentiation is what prevents self-cannibalisation.

Should each location have its own webpage?

Generally yes, once you have more than a couple of locations with meaningfully different service areas. For two closely related locations in the same metro area, a shared page with clear, genuinely differentiated sections can work. This is a case-by-case call based on how distinct each location's market actually is.

How is pricing structured for multi-location businesses?

As custom arrangements rather than a fixed per-location multiple, since the work depends on how many locations, how similar their markets are, and how much structural cleanup is needed first. We scope this during the free audit rather than quoting a number without seeing the actual accounts.

What information do you need from each location to get started?

Access to each location's Google Business Profile, confirmation of which locations are genuinely separate addresses versus service-area coverage from one base, and basic per-location details. Start with the free audit →
